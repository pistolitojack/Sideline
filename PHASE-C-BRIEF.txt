SIDELINE — PHASE C: MAKING THE REELS LOOK MADE
A briefing for a planning conversation. Written 2026-10-06.

I'm the founder — a former coach, not a programmer. Claude Code writes the code;
I direct it and judge the output on my phone. I want your honest read, including
if you think the plan below is wrong.

Everything in Part 2 is read out of the actual code, not remembered. Where I'm
guessing, I've said so.

===============================================================================
1. WHAT SIDELINE IS  (30 seconds)
===============================================================================

A coach films training on their phone, uploads the raw clips, and gets back
finished vertical reels — cut, captioned, and written — without touching an
editing app. They swipe to approve or skip, can send one back with a
plain-English note, then download and post manually.

Pipeline: ingest → DIRECTOR (plans what pieces to make) → transcribe (Deepgram)
→ understand (finds usable moments) → compose (picks the cuts, writes the copy)
→ render (ffmpeg) → revise.

The AI learns per coach. Every rejection, every typed note, every re-cut request
is stored and fed back into planning next time. That part demonstrably works: one
coach note — "the same clip played twice" — moved the next session's shortest
shot from 2.0s to 5.5s with no code change.

Two coaches have used it. Me, and my dad, who ran it for the first time
yesterday and approved 2 of 3 reels.

===============================================================================
2. WHAT THE RENDERER ACTUALLY DOES TODAY  (the important part)
===============================================================================

This is the honest inventory. It is smaller than people assume.

FONTS
  ONE. `/usr/share/fonts/truetype/dejavu/DejaVuSans-Bold.ttf`, hardcoded. It is
  the only font installed in the worker container. It was chosen because it was
  already there, not for any design reason.

CAPTION STYLES
  TWO, total:
    - hook:  54px, white, on a filled box in the coach's accent colour, y=1380
    - body:  42px, white, 6px black outline at 55% opacity, y=1470
  Both centred horizontally. Both static — they appear, hold, and disappear.
  Up to 3 wrapped lines each, hard-wrapped at 20 chars (hook) / 28 (body).

  Only ONE caption is on screen at a time. This is enforced in code, not by the
  model, because overlapping captions printed on top of each other.

TRANSITIONS
  Five: cut, fade, slideleft, slideright, circleopen. Chosen by the model per
  segment. Guidance is "mostly cuts and fades; at most 1-2 specialty wipes".

FRAMING
  Crop to 9:16, either centred or an eased horizontal pan. 1080x1920, CRF 21,
  6 Mbps cap.

AUDIO
  The original clip audio, loudness-normalised. Crossfades between segments.
  NO music, ever — licensing.

WHAT VARIES BETWEEN TWO DIFFERENT COACHES
  The accent colour. That is the entire list.

WHAT VARIES BETWEEN A TEACHING REEL AND A HYPE REEL
  Nothing. They are rendered pixel-identically.

===============================================================================
3. THE PROBLEM, STATED PLAINLY
===============================================================================

The AI writes differently for every coach. It does not LOOK different for any of
them.

Put ten coaches on this and their reels are visually interchangeable — same
typeface, same two caption treatments, same positions, same transitions. A coach
whose brand is loud and a coach whose brand is clinical get the same video with a
different accent colour.

For a product whose pitch is "an AI marketing employee that learns YOUR style",
that is the gap between what we claim and what we ship.

===============================================================================
4. WHAT I THINK PHASE C SHOULD BUILD
===============================================================================

Ordered by my guess at value per unit of risk. I want you to tell me if the order
is wrong.

--- C1. WORD-SYNCED CAPTIONS ------------------------------------------------

Captions that appear word by word, in time with the speech, rather than a block
that sits there for four seconds.

WHY THIS IS FIRST: it is the single most recognisable feature of modern
short-form video, and WE ALREADY HAVE THE DATA. Deepgram returns word-level
timings and we already store them (`punctuated_word`, start, end per word). We
wired them into the scorecard yesterday for a different reason. Nothing new has
to be captured, measured, or guessed.

HOW: almost certainly by generating ASS subtitle files and burning them with
ffmpeg's `ass` filter, rather than stacking more `drawtext` filters. ASS supports
karaoke timing, outlines, shadows, positioning and fades natively. The current
approach — one drawtext filter per caption, chained — does not animate and gets
unwieldy fast.

WHAT IT CHANGES FOR THE COACH: their reels stop looking like a slideshow with
subtitles and start looking like something a person edited.

--- C2. A CAPTION STYLE PER FORMAT -------------------------------------------

A teaching reel and a hype reel should not look the same. Roughly:
  teaching       calm, lower third, smaller, high legibility
  hype           big, centred, punchy, heavier outline
  story          minimal, almost no caption
  client win     testimonial framing, name/credit treatment

Today all four are identical. The piece kind is already stored on every piece
(`piece_kind`), so the renderer has the information and simply ignores it.

--- C3. REAL TYPEFACES ------------------------------------------------------

Install 3-5 open-licence fonts with genuinely different personalities and let the
coach pick one in onboarding, or let the director pick per format.

Low technical risk — fonts go in the Docker image and the path is already a
variable. The hard part is choosing well, not wiring it.

OPEN QUESTION FOR YOU: is "pick your font" a real brand control or a decision we
are offloading onto a coach who does not want it? Our north-star rule is that
every screen should REMOVE a decision, not add one.

--- C4. MOTION ON THE CUT ---------------------------------------------------

Punch-ins (a slow scale-up on a held shot), freeze frames at the peak of an
action, and speed ramps into a key moment.

These are what make a cut feel directed rather than merely correct. All three are
doable in ffmpeg. All three also carry real risk of looking cheap if applied
indiscriminately, which is why I have them below captions rather than above.

--- C5. SAFE AREAS ----------------------------------------------------------

Instagram and TikTok overlay their own UI on the bottom ~15% and right edge of
the frame. I do not know whether our captions at y=1380 and y=1470 (of 1920) sit
under it. If they do, every caption we have ever burned has been partly covered
on the platforms the reels are made for.

This is the cheapest item here and possibly the most embarrassing one. [CONFIRM]

===============================================================================
5. THE THING THAT WORRIES ME ABOUT THIS PHASE
===============================================================================

Everything we have built to keep ourselves honest measures STRUCTURE, not
AESTHETICS.

We have an automatic scorecard with nine checks — overlapping captions, a piece
too short, a shot too short or too long, repeated footage, a hook over eight
words, captions running past the end, a cut landing mid-word, a reel ending
mid-sentence. Every one of them is a yes/no fact read out of the edit list.

NONE of them can tell me whether a reel looks good.

That matters because of history. In September we stacked five changes to how the
AI decides, with no way to measure any of them, and the output got dramatically
worse — reels with text printed over text, two-second reels, copy describing a
different exercise than the video showed. It cost a month. The rule that came out
of it: never change how the AI decides without a way to measure whether it
helped.

Phase C is the first phase where that rule cannot be followed the same way. I can
count whether captions overlap. I cannot count whether a punch-in felt right.

QUESTIONS I HAVE ABOUT THIS:
  - Is there anything in the visual layer that IS objectively checkable, and that
    we should be detecting automatically? (Contrast ratio? Caption inside the
    safe area? Text-on-screen reading time versus word count?)
  - If not, what is the honest substitute — a fixed test set of clips re-rendered
    on every change and watched side by side?
  - Should Phase C ship behind a flag so one coach gets the new look and another
    gets the old one, and the approve/skip rates are compared?

===============================================================================
6. CONSTRAINTS
===============================================================================

- ffmpeg only. No After Effects, no Remotion, no headless browser rendering —
  unless you think one of those is worth the complexity, in which case say so.
- Render time matters. A session is 3-8 minutes end to end today and a coach is
  waiting. [CONFIRM: I have not measured what share of that is ffmpeg.]
- Cost matters. ~$0.35 per session today, ~94% of it the AI rather than compute.
- No music. Licensing.
- Mobile-first. Coaches review on a phone, one-handed, often at a gym.
- I am not a programmer. Whatever you suggest gets built by Claude Code, so
  "write a custom renderer" is a real cost, not a free suggestion.
- Two users so far, both related to me. Every claim about what coaches want,
  including mine, is a guess.

7. WHAT I'M ASKING YOU
===============================================================================

1. IS MY ORDER RIGHT? I have word-synced captions first because the data is
   already there and the effect is the most recognisable. Tell me if something
   else should go first, or if any of C1-C5 is not worth doing at all.

2. WHAT AM I MISSING? What does a professionally produced short-form reel have
   that is not on my list and that ffmpeg can actually do?

3. HOW DO I MEASURE AESTHETICS, OR DO I ACCEPT THAT I CAN'T? This is the question
   I most want answered. Section 5 is the real risk in this phase.

4. IS ASS THE RIGHT TOOL for animated captions, or is there a better path I
   don't know about?

5. SHOULD VISUAL STYLE BE A COACH CHOICE OR AN AI DECISION? Picking a font is a
   decision added to onboarding. Letting the director choose per format is a
   decision the AI can get wrong. Which is worse?

6. WHAT WOULD YOU NOT DO? If something on this list is a maintenance trap or
   will date badly, say so.

Please be direct. If an idea of mine is wrong, say it's wrong and show me why.
I'd much rather hear that than be agreed with and find out three sessions later —
that exact pattern cost this project a month in September.
