Read this first. The code snapshot follows separately.
Written 2026-09-13.

===============================================================================
WHY I'M ASKING
===============================================================================

I want to talk about how onboarding can collect BETTER DATA FOR THE AI.

Not shorter. Not longer. Better.

Onboarding is the very first thing a coach does, and everything they type
becomes the AI's entire understanding of who they are. If we get a strong
signal at the start, every reel the AI ever makes for that coach starts from a
better place. If the signal is thin or generic, the AI is guessing from day one
and no amount of later learning fully recovers it.

I'm not saying the current onboarding is bad. I want to know whether there's a
smarter way to do it — better questions, a different shape, something we
haven't thought of. Tell me if you'd do it differently, and tell me if you'd
leave it alone.

===============================================================================
WHAT SIDELINE IS  (30 seconds)
===============================================================================

An AI marketing employee for sports coaches. A coach uploads raw training
footage from their phone. The AI cuts it into finished vertical reels with
burned-in captions in their brand colour and writes the hook, caption, hashtags
and call-to-action. They swipe to approve or skip, can send a reel back with a
plain-English note, then download and post manually.

I'm the founder — a former coach, not a programmer. Claude Code writes the
code; I direct it and judge the output on my phone. Right now I'm the only
coach using it, which matters: everything below is tested on one person.

The pipeline: ingest → DIRECTOR (plans what pieces to make) → transcribe →
understand (finds moments) → compose (picks cuts, writes copy) → render.

===============================================================================
THE CURRENT ONBOARDING, STEP BY STEP
===============================================================================

Six screens, mobile-first. A signed-in user with no coach profile is sent here
automatically and can't reach the app until it's done.

  0. WELCOME — just a start button.

  1. INSTAGRAM HANDLE — optional. Followed by a deliberate "studying your page"
     pause. The handle is stored; a background job later scrapes the profile
     into a text summary of their existing brand.

  2. COACH DNA CARD — the main screen. Four things:
       • Name (free text)
       • Sport — ONE of: Speed & agility / Strength / Soccer / Basketball /
         Combat sports
       • Tones — any of: Direct / Encouraging / No-nonsense / Funny / Technical
       • Brand colour — one of four presets (Signal Red, Court Blue, Turf
         Green, Heat Orange)

  3. VOICE MEMO — up to 60 seconds, dark full-screen. They're shown one prompt:
       "What do most coaches get wrong about training young athletes?"
     and told: "Your employee copies how you sound — your rhythm and word
     choice — never what you say here."
     Recorded in the browser, sent to /api/transcribe (Deepgram), stored as
     text. Optional — they can skip it.

  4. MISSION — ONE of: More private clients / Fill the fall program / Build the
     brand / Sell online coaching. There's also a free-text box to write their
     own instead.

  5. CITY — free text. Told it "shapes location hashtags and audience tuning."

Then it saves and drops them into the app.

Two notes on how it's built: it's an UPSERT, so re-running onboarding updates
an existing profile rather than creating a duplicate. And changing the
Instagram handle deliberately wipes the stored profile summary so it gets
re-scanned.

===============================================================================
WHERE EACH FIELD ACTUALLY ENDS UP  (this is the important part)
===============================================================================

Onboarding is not a signup form. It IS the AI's model of the coach. Here is
exactly how many times each field is read by the AI worker, and where:

  sport                  5 uses   director + composer ("sport / focus: ...")
  mission                5 uses   director + composer ("mission right now: ...")
  ig_profile             5 uses   director + composer ("their Instagram brand: ...")
                                  — NOT typed by the coach; scraped from the handle
  voice_memo_transcript  4 uses   composer, as a STYLE sample
  audience               4 uses   director + composer ("audience: ...")
  name                   3 uses   composer
  tones                  2 uses   composer ("tones=Direct, Technical")
  accent_hex             2 uses   the render stage — burns the caption colour
  city                   1 use    director ("city: ...")
  ig_handle              2 uses   triggers the Instagram scan

The director sees this block before planning anything:

    THE COACH:
    - sport / focus: Speed & agility
    - audience: Youth athletes & their parents
    - mission right now: More private clients
    - city: Atlanta
    - their Instagram brand: <scraped summary>

And the composer, which writes every hook and caption, gets:

    Coach: name=Jack; sport=Speed & agility; tones=Direct, Technical;
    audience=Youth athletes & their parents; mission=More private clients.
    VOICE SAMPLE — copy STYLE only (tone, rhythm, word length), NEVER its
    topic or examples: "<the 60-second transcript>"
    Their real Instagram vibe: <scraped summary>

So a coach who picks generic chips gets generic reels. There is no other source
of truth about who they are.

===============================================================================
PROBLEMS FOUND — ordered by what they cost the AI
===============================================================================

These came out of reading the code line by line. I want a plan to fix them, and
I want to know which ones you think actually matter.

--- SEVERE: the AI is being told things that are not true -------------------

P1. AUDIENCE IS NEVER ASKED. IT IS HARDCODED.
    Every coach is saved as "Youth athletes & their parents". One line:
        audience: "Youth athletes & their parents",
    The AI reads it FOUR times and treats it as fact. A coach training adult
    fighters, college athletes or general-population clients is silently
    mislabelled, and every hook and CTA is aimed at people who aren't their
    customers. For me it happens to be true, which is why nothing looks broken.
    COST: wrong audience on every piece of copy, forever, invisibly.

P2. EVERY CHOICE IS PRE-SELECTED, SO TAPPING THROUGH PRODUCES CONFIDENT
    NONSENSE.
    The defaults are:
        sport   = "Speed & agility"
        tones   = ["Direct", "Encouraging"]
        mission = "More private clients"
        colour  = Signal Red
    A coach who taps Continue without changing anything is saved as a Direct,
    Encouraging Speed & Agility coach chasing private clients — whether or not
    a word of that is true. Only NAME is actually required.
    This is worse than leaving fields blank. A blank field reaches the AI as
    "sport / focus: ?" and it knows it doesn't know. A default reaches it as a
    fact and it reasons confidently from a lie.
    COST: the laziest possible path through onboarding produces data the AI
    cannot tell apart from a real answer.

--- REAL BUGS ---------------------------------------------------------------

P3. RE-RUNNING ONBOARDING WIPES THE INSTAGRAM SCAN, ALWAYS.
    The save does this unconditionally:
        ig_profile: null, // new handle → fresh scan on the next session
    The comment says "new handle", but the code doesn't check whether the
    handle changed. Fix a typo in your name and you lose the scraped summary of
    your Instagram brand — which the AI uses FIVE times — until a background
    job re-scrapes it.
    COST: a correct, expensive piece of profile data destroyed by an unrelated
    edit.

P4. THERE IS NO WAY BACK INTO ONBOARDING FROM THE APP.
    Nothing links to /onboarding. The only automatic redirect fires when a
    coach has no profile at all. To change your sport, your tones, your mission
    or re-record your voice memo, you have to know to type the URL. I hit this
    myself and had to ask how to get back in.
    COST: the profile is effectively write-once. Everything below about
    keeping it current is impossible in practice.

--- THIN SIGNAL: what we collect isn't as useful as it could be -------------

P5. THE VOICE MEMO'S CONTENT IS THROWN AWAY.
    We record 60 seconds of a coach answering "What do most coaches get wrong
    about training young athletes?" — then the composer prompt says:
        "copy STYLE only (tone, rhythm, word length), NEVER its topic or
         examples"
    So we keep the rhythm and discard the philosophy. It's the single richest
    thing a coach gives us and we use the least valuable half of it.

P6. TONES ARE ADJECTIVES, NOT EXAMPLES.
    "Direct, Technical" is a much weaker signal than one caption the coach
    actually wrote themselves. Five preset words are doing the work that a real
    sample of their writing would do far better.

P7. SPORT IS FIVE FIXED OPTIONS WITH NO ESCAPE HATCH.
    Speed & agility / Strength / Soccer / Basketball / Combat sports. No
    "other", no free text. A volleyball, swimming or track coach must pick
    something wrong, and the AI believes it.

P8. MISSION IS A SNAPSHOT THAT NEVER UPDATES.
    "Fill the fall program" is true in August and meaningless in January.
    Nothing ever asks again — and per P4, they can't easily change it anyway.

P9. NOTHING CAPTURES WHAT THE CONTENT IS FOR.
    We know their mission in four preset words. We don't know who they're
    actually trying to reach, what they sell, what a win looks like, or — maybe
    most useful of all — what they would NEVER post.

===============================================================================
WHAT HAPPENS AFTER ONBOARDING  (so you know what's already covered)
===============================================================================

We just finished a phase that makes the AI learn from behaviour, so onboarding
does NOT have to carry everything forever:

  - Every rejected reel records a reason + the coach's own words on what would
    have made it better
  - Every "send it back to the editor" note is stored verbatim
  - All of that is injected into the director's brief on later sessions
  - After each session the AI writes itself a note about what it learned

That system demonstrably works. One coach note — "the same clip played twice" —
changed the next session's shot lengths from 2.0s to 5.5s minimum.

So the honest framing is: ONBOARDING ONLY HAS TO GET THEM TO A GOOD FIRST
SESSION. The learning loop handles the rest. The question is what "good enough
to start" really needs, and whether we're collecting the right things for it.

===============================================================================
CONSTRAINTS
===============================================================================

- Mobile-first. Coaches do this on a phone, one-handed, probably at a gym.
- Every screen should remove a decision, not add one. That's the product's
  north-star rule.
- I'm not a programmer. Anything you suggest gets built by Claude Code.
- One coach so far (me). Any claim about "what coaches want" is a guess,
  including mine.
- Anything collected must actually reach the AI or it's dead weight — see the
  audience field, which is collected from nobody and used four times.

   rather keep a good thing than churn it.
